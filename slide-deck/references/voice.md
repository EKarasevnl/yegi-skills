# Voice and tone

Shared across every slide, demo, and one-pager.

## Absolute rules

**Never use em-dashes (—).** Not in body, callouts, diagrams, or demo narration. Replace with:
- a comma if the clause is short
- a period to split into two sentences
- a semicolon for closely related thoughts
- or restructure the sentence

This is the most-violated rule. Search the file for "—" before shipping.

**Do not address one person directly on a shared slide.** Decks run in rooms with several people. Prefer "the question raised earlier" over "your question". (A personal deck the user keeps for themselves can be written in their own voice.)

**No corporate buzzwords.** No "synergies", "leverage", "transform", "unlock value", "next-gen", "best-in-class", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "paradigm shift".

## Strong preferences

- **Simple, native English.** Short sentences, concrete nouns, verbs that do work.
- **Plain over fancy.** "Use" not "utilize". "Build" not "architect". "Show" not "demonstrate".
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the thing, the number, the date. Avoid "various initiatives".
- **Honest over polished.** If something is an idea, say idea. If it is not started, say "to do". Do not inflate.

## Patterns that work

- Two-part structure: state the thing, then what it does. "Pension · current plan and the reason for it."
- Trailing status tags: "to do", "in progress", "live", "to check". Status as a tag, not a paragraph.
- Mono keyword pairs separated by middle dots: "plan · reason · amount".
- Numbers with units and real precision: "+12% / month", "~€300 / month".

## Patterns to avoid

- "We're excited to announce", "we're thrilled to share".
- Sentences that say nothing: "This is critical to our success." Cut them.
- Rhetorical questions in body copy (an explicit "open question" callout is fine).
- Hedging: "Perhaps the most important...". Say what is important.

## Bad news and limitations

Be direct. Name the trade-off or failure mode plainly. Calling out a known risk by name earns trust faster than glossing over it.
